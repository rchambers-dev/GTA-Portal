 14. Regulator maintains constant voltage output by varying current to the rotor field coil.

 15. Provides electrical energy for starting and stabilises system voltage when charging demand is high.

 16. Alternator produces AC, rectified to DC, then sent to battery via main fuse and charge cable.

 17
 17. Maintains correct belt tension to prevent slippage and ensure efficient alternator operation.

 18
 18. Conventional claw-pole alternators and LIN-controlled smart alternators adjust output based on demand.

 19
 19. Lead plates in electrolyte (sulphuric acid) create chemical reaction generating voltage.

 20
 20. 12.6V = full charge, 12.2V = 50%, below 12V = discharged or faulty.

 21. Connect tester, apply load for 10 seconds, observe voltage drop; low voltage = weak battery.

 22. Measure output at battery terminals (13.8–14.5V normal); low = charging fault, high = regulator fault.

 23. Use clamp meter to read cranking current; high draw = mechanical fault, low draw = electrical fault.

 24. Measure voltage loss between terminals; more than 0.5V indicates excessive resistance.

 25. Measure resistance between alternator housing and battery negative; should be near zero ohms.

 29
 29. Turn ignition on; lamp should illuminate, then go out once charging starts; faults indicate wiring or regulator issues.
