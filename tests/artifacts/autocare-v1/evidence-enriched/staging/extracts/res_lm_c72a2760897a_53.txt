The Lubrication System 
 The lubrication system stores, cleans, cools, and circulates oil to lubricate moving engine parts and bearings. 
 Lubrication system parts include the oil pump, oil pan, oil cooler, oil filter, and oil galleries. 
 
 style.visibility style.visibility

Lubrication System Operation 
 The reservoir of oil is held in the oil sump. 
 Oil is drawn into the engine through a pickup and forced round the system by a pump 
 The system pressure is regulated by a pressure relief valve. 
 Particles are strained out by an oil filter. 
 Oil is circulated through the cylinder head and the engine block using galleries.

Dry Sump System 
 In a dry sump system, oil is stored in a separate storage tank and the oil sump is much smaller. 
 This allows the engine to be mounted lower down, which is ideal for racing cars. 
 A low-pressure ‘scavenge’ pump moves the oil from the sump to the tank. 
 The scavenge pump is bigger than the pressure pump, to ensure the sump is not over-filled.

 Engine Oil 
 Oil lubricates and cools the moving parts of an engine and reduces the formation of rust and corrosion. 
 It also acts as barrier to reduce friction and wear. 
 It is usually refined from petroleum or crude oil, but there are many synthetic (man made) oils available . 
 For a rotating bearing, the shaft diameter is less than the bearing inner diameter to allow space for an oil film.

Viscosity 
 Viscosity is the flow rate of oil, measured using the Society of Automotive Engineers ratings SAE 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 . 
 Low viscosity oil flows more easily and is better for cold (starting) operation. 
 High viscosity oil flows more slowly and is better when the engine is hot. 
 Most modern oils are mixtures, having multi-viscosity or multigrade to cope with a wide range of engine temperatures.

Mineral, Synthetic and semi-synthetic oil 
 
 Most motor oils are made from crude oil, with additives to improve certain properties. These are known as mineral oils. 
 
 Synthetic oil is a lubricant consisting of chemical compounds that are artificially made. Synthetic lubricants can be manufactured using chemically modified petroleum components rather than whole crude oil but can also be synthesized from other raw materials. Synthetic oil is used as a substitute for petroleum-refined oils when operating in extreme temperature. 
 
 Semi-synthetic oils are a mixture of mineral oil and synthetic oil, with no more than 30% synthetic oil, which are engineered to have many of the benefits of full synthetic oil without the cost.

The Oil Pump 
 The oil pump is usually located on the bottom of the engine block. 
 It is driven either from the crankshaft by a chain or the camshaft by a gear arrangement. 
 The oil pump pressurizes and pumps oil to moving parts of the engine. 
 The main two types of pump are rotor and gear.

Oil Galleries 
 Passages through the engine block and cylinder head, called galleries, carry oil to all parts of the engine. 
 This engine has the camshaft and many valve train components, all needing lubrication. 
 Cylinders are lubricated to allow the pistons to move freely up and down. 
 Galleries are used to carry oil to and from the oil filter. 
 A large diameter tube joins the pickup to the oil pump.

The Oil Filter 
 Oil filters are used to strain the engine oil to remove abrasive particles. 
 They are usually a ‘spin-on’ fitting with an O-ring seal to prevent leakage. 
 The paper element traps small metal, rust, carbon, or dirt particles.

The Oil Cooler 
 Oil coolers are sometimes used to control engine oil temperature. 
 A heat exchanger similar to a radiator is used to transfer the heat to the outside air. 
 Hoses and lines are usually connected to an adapter on the oil filter housing. 
 Oil coolers are normally only used for heavy-duty applications, such as turbocharged engines or trailer towing vehicles.

Cleaning 
 It is inevitable that small particles of metal will erode inside the engine. 
 There is also carbon, water and acids produced by the combustion that will dilute the oil and cause deterioration. 
 These are collected by the oil. 
 The oil filter removes most of these from the oil but eventually the oil has to be changed. Used oil is carcinogenic, that means it can cause cancer, so protect your skin accordingly.

Testing oil pressure 
 
 First ensure that the engine is cool enough to work on. Apply the handbrake, chock the wheels and put the transmission in neutral or park. 
 
 Now locate the oil pressure or temperature sender – situated in the engine block and often near the sump. 
 
 Remove the switch and be prepared with an oil drip tray to catch any oil. 
 
 style.visibility style.visibility

Testing oil pressure 
 
 An Oil Pressure Test Kit has a selection of adaptors to suit a variety of thread patterns, so find the one that matches your engine and fix this into the block where the oil pressure sender was removed. 
 
 Now attach the oil pressure gauge to the adaptor and check the engine oil level before starting the vehicle and allowing it to reach operating temperature. 
 
 style.visibility style.visibility

Testing oil pressure 
 
 Consult a service manual to determine what RPM the test should be performed at, and what pressure your engine should maintain. If necessary, have an assistant hold the engine at this RPM while you take a reading. 
 
 A low reading could indicate a blocked oil filter, worn oil pump, restricted oil pickup - or simply a grade of oil that is too thin for the engine. A high reading could be due to a faulty pressure relief valve or other blockages.

Testing oil pressure 
 
 Once you have taken the reading, switch the engine off and allow it to cool before removing the oil pressure test gauge, and re-installing the oil pressure sensor. 
 
 Top up the oil as necessary – and the job is complete.
