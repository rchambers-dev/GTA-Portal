 Topic and intended learning
 Lesson aims - Develop secure occupational knowledge and safe working practice for suspension component replacement and alignment preparation. - Connect the session to workplace quality, communication and customer/business impact. - Produce clear evidence that can be reviewed and improved.
 By the end, apprentices should be able to - Explain the key principles, sequence, risks and controls for suspension component replacement and alignment preparation. - Complete the planned practical, scenario or documentation activity safely and accurately. - Record findings and justify decisions using technical information or workplace procedures.
 How learning will be checked - Baseline and retrieval questions at the start. - Observation, questioning and review during activity. - Feedback, correction and an end-of-session impact check. - Formal assessment, gateway and EPA activity remain separate.
 Inclusion, support and stretch - Use visual prompts, glossaries, staged checklists and model examples where needed. - Adjust access arrangements without lowering occupational expectations. - Stretch: justify alternatives, evaluate risk/quality and explain workplace impact.
 RPL and individual training plan control - Deliver the full master plan unless an approved RPL adjustment changes the learner’s block plan. - Record only the eligible OTJ actually delivered. Omitted or shortened learning must agree with Excel and the portal.
 Impact and follow-up - Identify what the apprentice can now explain or complete with greater confidence and independence. - Record one workplace application or evidence action. - Review progress against the block objectives and agree the next step.

 Detailed theory session covering suspension system operation and component replacement procedures. Tutor explains function of springs, dampers, control arms and bushes, and how these components influence vehicle stability, braking and tyre wear.
 Baseline questions; correct misconceptions. Support with visuals/glossary. Stretch: connect principles to workplace risk and quality.

 Structured practical activity inspecting suspension components on training vehicles. Learners identify components, check for wear, damage and movement, and follow a structured inspection routine.
 Observe process and safe working. Support with staged checklist. Stretch: justify method and compare alternatives.
 Tyre and alignment equipment; TPMS tools; gauges; PPE

 12:45-14:15 1.50 OTJ
 Application activity diagnosing suspension-related faults based on scenarios such as knocking noises, uneven tyre wear, poor ride quality or vehicle instability. Learners must identify likely causes, link symptoms to specific components and recommend appropriate repairs.
 Review output and reasoning. Support with model examples. Stretch: evaluate quality, efficiency and customer/business impact.
 Tyre and alignment equipment; TPMS tools; gauges; PPE

 Consolidation through discussion reviewing diagnostic decisions and reinforcing safe repair practices. Tutor-led questioning checks understanding of component function, fault impact and correct repair procedures.
