Introduction This presentation covers the types of inspection and maintenance offered by many garages today. Inspection and maintenance is usually known as ‘servicing’ and is necessary to keep a vehicle operating efficiently, safely and within certain legal legislation. Failure to have a vehicle serviced regularly can result in premature wear of various components and can sometimes lead to vehicle breakdowns and costly repairs. It is bad practice to neglect having a vehicle regularly serviced. Even if finances are tight, a basic minimum should still be carried out in order to at least renew essential lubricants and inspect components and systems to ensure the vehicle is operating correctly and safely. Initially we will discuss the types of inspection and servicing offered by garages.

Safety Check Safety checks are occasionally offered by garages during specific seasons e.g. winter or summer. The safety check is a basic low cost check to determine that the vehicle is roadworthy. Generally no parts are changed during a safety check however necessary fluids are topped up and tyre pressures are set. A report is usually issued to the customer to inform of areas of their vehicle which might need attention. Occasionally a safety check is offered at the same time as an MOT test, basically to give a customer peace of mind that their vehicle is roadworthy.

Pre Delivery Inspection A pre delivery inspection (PDI) is carried out at a franchised main dealer garage where new vehicles are sold and is the inspection of a new vehicle to ensure that it is satisfactory for delivery to the customer. On a new vehicle, everything is checked to ensure that it is operating correctly. All relevant adjustments are done and fluid levels and tyre pressures are checked and corrected. An inspection sheet is completed by the technician and the relevant details are entered in the vehicle service book. 
 style.visibility

Pre Sales Inspection A pre sales inspection is generally carried out at a garage offering used vehicles for sale. For a used vehicle, a pre sales inspection varies from a basic check over of the vehicle systems and fluid levels, up to a ‘full’ service. Some garages deliver their vehicles fully serviced because the customer can be confident that their new vehicle has been fully checked and also if the vehicle has been sold with a warranty it is usually a condition that the vehicle is sold fully serviced. If the vehicle sold is due for its MOT test, this will often be included as part of the pre sales inspection. 
 style.visibility

Interim Service An interim service is sometimes carried out in between the vehicle manufacturer’s full service periods. Eg , if the full service interval is 12000 miles or 12 months, an interim service may be carried out at 6000 miles or 6 months. An interim service covers a basic check of the vehicle operating systems, steering, suspension and brakes. All fluid levels and tyre pressures are checked, topped up and set. The engine oil and filter may also be renewed. Because of advancements in vehicle technology and materials, some vehicle manufacturers now delete the interim service from their schedules although for vehicles covering a high annual mileage or operating in extreme conditions it is still good practice to have this service carried out . 
 style.visibility

Full service A full service is carried out at a point where the vehicle manufacturer considers that the replaceable parts will be at the end of their useful life. The routine covers the following: Full check of the vehicle operating systems, steering and suspension. The brakes are usually inspected and cleaned. All fluid levels are topped up and tyre pressures checked and set. All the renewable filters (oil, air, pollen and fuel) are changed and the engine oil and gearbox oil are renewed. For petrol engines, the spark plugs are renewed and the running and fuel emissions settings of the engine may be checked. The wheel alignment may also be checked and adjusted as necessary. Finally the vehicle is road tested to ensure that all is satisfactory and the service record is completed. As a courtesy to their customers, some garages also valet the vehicle following a full service. Dependant upon the vehicle manufacturer’s recommendations, a full service interval is usually between 12000 and 30000 miles or yearly to every two years.

MOT Test A light vehicle is generally due for its first MOT test when reaches three years old. When a vehicle has reached this age it is a legal requirement to have a current, valid MOT certificate to be able to drive and insure the vehicle. The MOT test is carried out to Ministry of Transport standards to ensure that a vehicle is roadworthy and meets set safety criteria. The test is carried out at garages which have been approved by the Vehicle Operating Standards Agency (VOSA) and by operatives who have received appropriate training and have also been approved by VOSA.
