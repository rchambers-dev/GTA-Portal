Servotronic Power Steering 
 Most hydraulic power steering systems offer a constant level of assistance. 
 At low speeds there is more assistance to make maneuvering easier. 
 Servotronic systems can vary the amount of assistance according to the speed of the vehicle. 
 Next > 
 At high speeds there is less assistance, so the vehicle is more stable.

Servotronic Operation 
 At low speeds the ECU keeps the solenoid valve shut to maximize the hydraulic pressure for steering assistance. 
 Servotronic adds an ECU-controlled solenoid valve to the hydraulic power steering control valve. 
 Next > 
 As speed builds, the ECU uses a PWM signal to gradually open the solenoid. This makes the pressurized fluid bypass the control valve.

Electric Power Steering (EPS) 
 Hydraulic power steering systems are heavy, absorb engine power, and are prone to leaks. 
 An electric motor assists the steering but only when required. This increases fuel economy because it is not constantly absorbing engine power. 
 Many modern vehicles use electric power steering (EPS) because it is lighter, quicker to manufacture, and easier to maintain.

Power Steering Motor Drive 
 Column assist and pinion assist layouts use a reduction gearbox with a worm gear on the motor shaft. 
 If the motor is mounted on the rack housing, a belt takes the drive from the motor to a worm and nut on the steering rack. 
 The high rotational speed of an electric motor needs to be reduced in order to drive a steering system.

Steering Torque Sensor 
 The steering torque sensor is mounted at the bottom of the steering column. 
 As steering torque is applied, the input and output shafts twist relative to each other. 
 The input shaft and output shaft are connected by a thinner, flexible shaft.

Self-Centering 
 The geometry of a vehicle steering system makes it naturally self-center ‘hands off’ after turning a corner. 
 A disadvantage of electric power steering is that it will not readily do this. 
 Next > 
 The resistance caused by the gearing means it takes a large amount of force from the steering mechanism to make the motor turn.

Electric Power Steering Operation 
 Although the feedback force is artificial, the ECU is programmed to replicate the feel of conventional steering. 
 Next > 
 The limitation is the inertia of the motor when it has to switch between spinning one way and spinning the other. 
 On some vehicles the steering ECU has different programs, such as a ‘city’ mode that requires less steering effort.

Automatic Parallel Parking 
 Although the EPS motor is designed to add to the driver’s steering input, it can also work on its own. 
 EPS systems have also made it easier to develop fully autonomous ‘driverless’ vehicles. 
 Some modern cars are equipped with a system for automatic parallel parking without steering input from the driver. 
 Next > 
 They use sensors to work out the proximity of other objects.
