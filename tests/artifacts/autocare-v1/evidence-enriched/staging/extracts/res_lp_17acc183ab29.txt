 Topic and intended learning
 Lesson aims - Develop secure occupational knowledge and safe working practice for alignment, ride height and geometry correlation. - Connect the session to workplace quality, communication and customer/business impact. - Produce clear evidence that can be reviewed and improved.
 By the end, apprentices should be able to - Explain the key principles, sequence, risks and controls for alignment, ride height and geometry correlation. - Complete the planned practical, scenario or documentation activity safely and accurately. - Record findings and justify decisions using technical information or workplace procedures.
 How learning will be checked - Baseline and retrieval questions at the start. - Observation, questioning and review during activity. - Feedback, correction and an end-of-session impact check. - Formal assessment, gateway and EPA activity remain separate.
 Inclusion, support and stretch - Use visual prompts, glossaries, staged checklists and model examples where needed. - Adjust access arrangements without lowering occupational expectations. - Stretch: justify alternatives, evaluate risk/quality and explain workplace impact.
 RPL and individual training plan control - Deliver the full master plan unless an approved RPL adjustment changes the learner’s block plan. - Record only the eligible OTJ actually delivered. Omitted or shortened learning must agree with Excel and the portal.
 Impact and follow-up - Identify what the apprentice can now explain or complete with greater confidence and independence. - Record one workplace application or evidence action. - Review progress against the block objectives and agree the next step.

 09:30-10:30 1.00 OTJ
 Tutor-led technical session exploring the relationship between alignment, ride height and vehicle geometry. The tutor explains how camber, caster, toe, thrust angle and ride height influence steering feel, tyre wear, straight-line stability and braking behaviour.
 Baseline questions; correct misconceptions. Support with visuals/glossary. Stretch: connect principles to workplace risk and quality.
 Tyre and alignment equipment; TPMS tools; gauges; PPE

 10:45-12:15 1.50 OTJ
 Guided practical activity using alignment equipment, vehicle examples or alignment printouts. Learners review pre-checks including tyre condition, tyre pressure, wheel security, suspension condition, ride height and visible damage.
 Observe process and safe working. Support with staged checklist. Stretch: justify method and compare alternatives.
 Tyre and alignment equipment; TPMS tools; gauges; PPE

 Scheduled lunch.
 Excluded from OTJ.

 12:45-14:15 1.50 OTJ
 Application activity where learners diagnose geometry-related scenarios such as pulling, steering wheel off-centre, uneven tyre wear or handling complaints.
 Review output and reasoning. Support with model examples. Stretch: evaluate quality, efficiency and customer/business impact.
 Tyre and alignment equipment; TPMS tools; gauges; PPE
 14:15-14:30 Break

 14:30-16:00 1.50 OTJ
 Consolidation through review of alignment findings, diagnostic reasoning and customer explanation. The tutor questions learners on the relationship between geometry and vehicle behaviour, challenging any conclusions that are not supported by evidence.
 Exit check and feedback. Confirm evidence action. Stretch: explain improvement and transfer to the workplace.
 Evidence: Alignment or tyre inspection sheets; torque records; handover notes
 Workplace follow-up Apply one aspect of the session in the workplace and retain a job card, observation, photograph, mentor comment or other suitable evidence where available.
 Trainer preparation and delivery control Check the work area, PPE/RPE, tools/equipment, technical information, learner support needs and evidence location. Confirm any approved RPL adjustment before delivery.
 Assessment and exclusions Practical observation and peer QA (formative practice with feedback; training only). Separate formal assessment: none.
