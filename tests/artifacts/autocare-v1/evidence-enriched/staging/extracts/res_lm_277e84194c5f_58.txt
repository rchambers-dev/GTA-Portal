Introduction 
 The electrical systems on a vehicle require electrical power so that they can function. 
 When an engine is stationary, electrical power is provided by a battery. 
 When an engine is running, electrical power is provided by an alternator. 
 Next > 
 The starter motor turns to start an engine. This requires the largest amount of power, so good battery maintenance is essential.

Battery Cell Chemistry 
 Next > 
 In a lead acid battery cell, the electrolyte is a mixture of sulfuric acid (H 2 SO 4 ) and water. 
 The positive plate contains lead peroxide (PbO 2 ). 
 The negative plate contains porous, spongy lead (Pb).

Battery Cell – Discharging 
 Next > 
 When a load is connected across the plates, a chemical reaction causes electricity to flow around the circuit. 
 Sulfate ions in the electrolyte combine with both plates to form lead sulfate, releasing electrons from the negative plate terminal. 
 Oxygen from the positive plate combines with hydrogen in the electrolyte, forming water.

 As the current flows, the electrolyte and current become weaker until the battery is fully discharged.

Battery Cell – Charging 
 Next > 
 Sulfate ions leave both plates and combine with the hydrogen in the water to re-form sulfuric acid. 
 Oxygen, released by the water in the electrolyte, combines with the lead in the positive plate to re-form lead peroxide. 
 When a DC voltage higher than the battery voltage is connected across the plates, current flows into it. 
 = electrons 
 The negative plate turns back to a spongy lead.

 The cold cranking amps (CCA) rating shows how much current is provided by the battery for an industry standard of 30 seconds at -18 °C while the battery voltage remains above 7.2 V. 
 The reserve capacity is the amount of time the battery can supply an industry standard of 25 A at 27 °C before the individual cell voltages drop below 1.7 V.

 Most often the case is made of polypropylene (plastic) or a hard rubber compound. 
 Next > 
 It is light, strong, and molded with thin walls and cell dividers. The number of cell dividers determine how many cells the battery has. 
 Ribs are molded into the bottom of each cell cavity to collect sediment that sheds from the plates as they gradually deteriorate.

Nickel Metal Hydride Batteries 
 Next > 
 The nickel metal hydride (NiMH) rechargeable battery has generally replaced the nickel-cadmium (NiCd) battery. 
 It suffers less from the ‘memory effect’ problem than NiCd batteries. 
 NiMH batteries have a lower internal resistance than non-rechargeable batteries, making them ideal for high current applications. 
 They are relatively cheap to produce and easy to recharge but lose 20 % of charge a month.

Lithium-ion Batteries 
 During charging, the temperature of early lithium-ion batteries could rise high enough to release dangerous gases. 
 Next > 
 For this reason, the lithium used in modern batteries is combined with other elements, such as cobalt. 
 A typical lithium-ion battery cell is rated at 3.6 V, which is three times more than a NiMH cell (1.2 V).
