Introduction

A technician that works on electric vehicles needs to be suitably qualified.

Next >

There are special procedures for working on high voltage electric vehicles.

High voltage electricity is dangerous and can cause burns, shocks, sparks, explosions, and fire. It must be treated with respect.

===== SLIDE 6 =====
Personal Protective Equipment - PPE

Before any work with high voltage vehicles is carried out, it is important to have the correct PPE.

Next >

Insulating rubber gloves should be worn when working on HV systems.

PPE should include a non-conductive face shield to protect against chemical splashes and debris caused by HV arcing.

These offer protection up to 1000 V.

===== SLIDE 7 =====
Insulating Gloves

Always check the insulating gloves before starting work.

Next >

If you think there may be a problem, blow into the glove and seal the air inside.

Then press the glove to increase pressure inside the glove and check for air leaks.

If a glove leaks, it should be discarded.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
Other PPE

Rubber soled boots are recommended for working on HV systems.

The rubber sole provides a high resistance, non-conductive barrier between the wearer and the ground.

Next >

Non-conductive footwear for work on HV systems

Non-synthetic clothing  is recommended for working on HV systems.

This is because synthetic clothing, such as nylon, could melt at high temperatures.

===== SLIDE 11 =====
Safety Precautions

Next >

There are certain safety precautions that can be taken before disabling the high voltage system:

Create a high voltage buffer zone around the vehicle

Place a sign on the roof to warn others of the high voltage hazard

Position a suitable fire extinguisher in a nearby location

===== SLIDE 12 =====
Working on High Voltage Systems

All tasks on the high voltage system require it to be disabled.

Next >

To do this, remove the service plug or open the safety switch.

Wait five minutes for all energy storage devices, such as capacitors, to discharge.

Use a multimeter, certified to 1000 V, to check there is no stored charge remaining.

===== SLIDE 13 =====
Testing for High Voltage Residue

High voltage circuits do not use the chassis ground so readings must be taken directly at high voltage connections.

Next >

Readings may be taken at the high voltage relays that link the battery pack to the HV system.

Readings may also be taken in the engine compartments between HV connections.

Working on High Voltage Systems

Cleaning sprays should not be used on HV circuits because these may make the surrounding air conductive.

Next >

Reduce the risk of electric shock by using the one-hand rule when working on a HV circuit.

Do not back-probe HV connectors because this will damage them.
