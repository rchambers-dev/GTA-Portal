Introduction

Brake lines and hoses are an essential part of the brake system.

Problems with lines and hoses can lead to total brake failure, so they must be well maintained.

Brake System

From a safety point of view, the brake system is the most important system on a vehicle.

Though it is often simple to replace brake lines, hoses and fittings, care must be taken to ensure they will function correctly.

===== SLIDE 5 =====
Hydraulic Circuit Defects

The most common defects found in brake lines, hoses and fittings are leaks.

Leaks can usually be detected by a visual inspection.

Next >

A leak from a line, hose or fitting MUST be corrected in order to prevent possible brake failure.

===== SLIDE 6 =====
Brake Line Construction

Whenever possible, steel brake lines are used for the hydraulic system.

Steel lines are resistant to corrosion, collision damage, and vibration.

Next >

They can withstand high hydraulic pressure and are relatively inexpensive.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
Flexible Brake Hose Construction

Flexible hoses are usually made from rubber and synthetic fabric.

Typically, two plies of natural rubber and braided fabric material are surrounded by a synthetic rubber outer sheath.

Next >

The braided material gives added resistance to internal pressure.

The synthetic rubber outer sheath helps to reduce damage from atmospheric pollutants.

Brake Line Maintenance

Steel brake lines can be damaged by road debris, or may clog internally.

Should a steel line show signs of damage, especially kinking or crushing, it must be replaced.

===== SLIDE 14 =====
Brake Hose Problems

Flexible hoses can develop internal leaks, which will show up as swellings or bulges.

You should also check flexible hoses for signs of road damage or cracking of the outer covering.

Next >

In both cases the hose should be replaced.

===== SLIDE 15 =====
Blocked Brake Hose

When a hose swells internally, the restriction can cause the related wheel’s brake to apply slowly or, in extreme cases, not at all.

When braking, the reduced pressure on one wheel will cause the vehicle to pull to the opposite side.

Blocked Brake Hose

The hose can also swell to the extent that it prevents fluid returning.

This stops the cylinder or caliper pistons from returning when the brake pedal is released.

===== SLIDE 17 =====
Brake Fittings Maintenance

Fittings are generally trouble-free, but they can be damaged by road debris or become clogged inside.

If a fitting is damaged or leaking it should be replaced.

Next >

===== SLIDE 18 =====
New Brake Lines

A new brake line must closely match the length and shape of the old line.

To fit properly, the new line must first be bent to shape. Then, if required, it should be cut and flared using appropriate tools.

Next >

To prevent kinks in the line, the proper tools must be used to make tight bends.

Lever bender

===== SLIDE 19 =====
Fabricating Brake Lines

Brake lines must be flared to ensure a leak-proof connection.

Next >

Double-Lap Flare

ISO Flare

There are two standard types of flares:

A double-lap flare requires two steps to produce and uses a flaring bar and flaring yoke.

An ISO flare requires a special tool that contains a tool body, collet, forming mandrel, clamping nut and forming screw.

===== SLIDE 20 =====
Replacing Hoses and Fittings

Replacement hoses must match the old hose, be securely fitted, and be bled after being fitted.

Some fittings are part of larger assemblies that must be entirely replaced if the fittings are defective.

Next >

Others can be replaced without affecting other parts, but the entire system must be bled after fitting.
