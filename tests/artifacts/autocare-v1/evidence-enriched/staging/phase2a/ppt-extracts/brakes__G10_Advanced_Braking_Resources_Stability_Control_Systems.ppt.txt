===== SLIDE 2 =====
Introduction

Electronic stability control (ESC) is a safety system that helps to prevent the driver losing control of the vehicle.

Very little additional equipment is needed, so it is cheap to implement.

It uses elements of the anti-lock braking system (ABS) and traction control system (TCS).

Next >

Studies show that it could reduce accidents by up to 35 %.

===== SLIDE 4 =====
ESC Situation Monitoring

The ESC constantly monitors driver inputs, such as steering, brake pedal travel and accelerator pedal travel.

If the ESC detects a discrepancy between driver inputs and vehicle movement, it intervenes.

It also determines the movement of the vehicle by measuring wheel speeds, yaw and acceleration.

===== SLIDE 5 =====
Detecting Understeer and Oversteer

If the ESC detects that the vehicle is not yawing enough to match the steering input for a corner, the vehicle is understeering.

Next >

If it detects that the vehicle is yawing too much, the vehicle is oversteering.

===== SLIDE 6 =====
ESC Intervention

The ESC can apply or release brakes on individual wheels to make the vehicle yaw.

Next >

It can also affect the engine power or shift an automatic transmission to correct the situation.

It may apply an inside rear brake to correct understeer.

It may apply an outside front brake to correct oversteer.

===== SLIDE 7 =====
ESC Components and Sensors

Many components of an ESC system are existing components of the ABS and TCS.

It also uses acceleration and yaw sensors, which are based on miniature integrated circuits.

ESC adds more sensors, such as steering wheel angle and brake pedal position.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
Human Factors

ESC works automatically, and the driver may not even be aware that it has been operating.

Consequently, the ESC will inform the driver, visually or audibly, that it has had to intervene.

There is a risk that the driver may become complacent and believe their skill level to be higher than it actually is.

===== SLIDE 9 =====
Disabling the ESC

There is usually a switch on the dashboard that will partially or fully disable the ESC.

The driver may also use this if they want to deliberately drive the vehicle beyond the limit of available grip.

Next >

For safety, this should not be done on the public highway.

Manufacturers often recommend switching off the ESC in deep snow.
