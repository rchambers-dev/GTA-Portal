Wheel Speed Sensor Function

A wheel speed sensor detects the speed of rotation of a wheel.

The sensor is positioned close to the tone wheel with an air gap in between.

It works in conjunction with a toothed tone wheel that is fitted to the hub or CV joint or axle.

===== SLIDE 4 =====
Inductive Wheel Speed Sensor Construction

A typical inductive sensor contains an internal coil or winding that is wound around a permanent magnet.

As the tone wheel rotates, each passing tooth causes a rapid change in the magnetic field that induces an AC voltage in the coil.

This produces a magnetic field around the coil.

Next >

The frequency and amplitude of the AC voltage signal varies in relation to the speed of the wheel.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
Anti-lock Brake ECU

The AC output signal from the sensor is sent to the ABS ECU; it uses the information to calculate the degree of slip between the wheels and the road surface.

If this is the case, the ECU intervenes to prevent the wheels from locking by holding or reducing hydraulic pressure to the affected brake units.

On an ABS system, this is used to determine if any of the wheels are about to lock (negative wheel slip).

===== SLIDE 9 =====
Traction Control ECU

On a traction control system, the sensor signal is used to determine if any of the wheels are about to spin (positive wheel slip).

If this does not reduce positive wheel slip, the ECU will reduce engine power by taking control of the throttle valve or reducing the number of firing cylinders.

The TCS ECU prevents the wheels from spinning by holding or increasing hydraulic pressure to the affected brake units.

Hall Effect Wheel Speed Sensors

The Hall Effect sensor is designed to detect the presence of a magnetic field.

The magnetic field penetrates the sensor and the tone wheel.

A typical sensor uses a magnet and tone wheel arrangement.

===== SLIDE 14 =====
Hall Effect Wheel Speed Sensor Operation

As the tone wheel rotates, it disrupts the magnetic field detected by the Hall Effect sensor.

The frequency of the digital signal depends on the speed of the tone wheel while the amplitude of the signal remains constant.

The sensor produces a corresponding digital output that changes between + 5 V and 0 V.

Next >

This system is capable of detecting zero wheel speed.

===== SLIDE 17 =====
Magnetic Encoder Ring Systems

On some systems, the tone wheel is constructed using magnets with alternating poles arranged in a ring.

The sensing system contains two Hall Effect sensors that produce signals that are out of phase.

As the tone wheel rotates, the sensing system detects alternating north and south magnetic fields.

Next >

This allows the system to detect zero wheel speed and direction of rotation.

===== SLIDE 18 =====
Wheel Speed Sensor Testing

The ABS ECU tests the resistance of each inductive type wheel speed sensor circuit when the ignition is first switched on.

A wiring short will typically show up as a very low resistance.

The resistance of a typical sensor is 1,000 ohms.

Next >

A broken wire or open circuit sensor will show up as a high resistance.

===== SLIDE 19 =====
Wheel Speed Sensor Testing

The ABS ECU tests the output signal from each wheel speed sensor circuit when the vehicle reaches 12 to 15 mph.

A faulty wheel speed sensor will cause the ABS ECU to illuminate the ABS warning indicator and store a diagnostic trouble code.
