===== SLIDE 6 =====
Fixed Callipers

The position of the calliper is fixed in relation to the brake disc.

When pressure is released the seals return to their original shape, forcing the pistons back to their starting positions.

As hydraulic pressure is applied, the pistons are forced inwards against the disc, deforming the seals around them.

Next >

A typical calliper assembly has two pistons, one on each side of the disc.

Floating Callipers

===== SLIDE 11 =====
Rear Brake Callipers

The design of brake callipers mounted on rear wheels is slightly different from those of the front.

Nearly all rear brake callipers are of the single piston floating design.

Rear callipers are smaller than front callipers because the rear brakes do not have to generate as much braking force.

===== SLIDE 14 =====
Piston Seals

The calliper piston moves against the square cross-section seal.

Next >

As the brakes are applied and the piston extends, this ring is deformed.

When the brakes are released, the ring pushes the piston back in and returns to its original shape.

This provides a running clearance for the pads, preventing the brake pads from binding on the disc.

===== SLIDE 15 =====
Calliper Maintenance

Two common types of problems associated with brake callipers are:

Next >

External brake fluid leaks, shown by brake fluid on the calliper housing, disc, piston, or dust boot.

Sticking pistons, shown by a brake that will not apply or release.

A leaking or sticking calliper must be overhauled or replaced.

Dust boot

===== SLIDE 16 =====
Calliper Piston Maintenance

After inspection, a faulty calliper piston can be removed using compressed air.

Next >

All piston parts should be cleaned in a non-petroleum cleaner and blown dry.

Usually, most pistons only require new seals and dust boots for a successful overhaul.

Sometimes, it may be  necessary to replace the piston or the whole calliper if the cylinder bore has been damaged.
