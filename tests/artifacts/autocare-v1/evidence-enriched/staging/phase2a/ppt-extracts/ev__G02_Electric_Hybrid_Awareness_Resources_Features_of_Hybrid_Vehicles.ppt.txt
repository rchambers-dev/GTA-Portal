===== SLIDE 7 =====
Stop-Start Technology

Next >

Modern engines used in hybrid vehicles often have the alternator and the starter motor combined into a single compact unit.

The engine stops when the vehicle stops, for example, at a red light.

This reduces the amount of fuel used.

In some vehicles the engine also stops during coasting and braking, reducing fuel use further.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
Stop-Start Technology

Next >

The engine can be restarted by either releasing the brake, depressing the clutch, or depressing the accelerator.

The battery power is used through the alternator/starter to re-start the engine.

Over 40 % of new vehicles incorporate stop-start technology as an easy and cost effective way to make savings in both fuel use and emissions.

===== SLIDE 9 =====
Boosting

Next >

Both mild hybrids and full hybrids are able to boost the drive capability when accelerating or operating under load.

Mild hybrids boost the conventional engine  with the electric motor.

Full hybrids boost the electric motor with the conventional engine.

In both cases, the microprocessor system determines the most efficient configuration for the conditions.

===== SLIDE 10 =====
Regenerative Braking

Next >

Conventional vehicles are fitted with hydraulic brakes.

When the brakes are applied, the vehicle converts the kinetic energy to heat.

This heat is wasted because it heats up the surrounding atmosphere.

Hybrid and electric vehicles are fitted with regenerative brakes.

===== SLIDE 11 =====
Regenerative Braking

Next >

Regenerative braking uses an electric motor as a generator to capture the kinetic energy, charge the battery, and slow the vehicle.

The electric motor is reconfigured by the microprocessor to become a generator.

In some vehicles, regenerative braking can also be activated when the gas pedal is released, or when a vehicle is traveling downhill.
