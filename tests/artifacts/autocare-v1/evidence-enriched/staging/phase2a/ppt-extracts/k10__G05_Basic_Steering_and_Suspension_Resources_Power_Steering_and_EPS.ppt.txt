===== SLIDE 5 =====
Hydraulic Power Steering SystemThis system is functional, but has design flaws:

The pump needs power from the engine to drive it. We call this a Parasitic loss. It gives max flow/pressure to the steering system when its least needed.It gives the least flow/pressure when most needed. For example, Parking.Its bulky, prone to leaks and can lack feel. Maintenance is also required. Its no good for modern stop/start systems.

===== SLIDE 6 =====
Hydraulic power Steering System

To try to rectify these short comings the system needed to be modified.This led to the introduction of the electro-hydraulic power steering system.

===== SLIDE 7 =====
Electro-Hydraulic Power Steering

Electro-Hydraulic power steering uses an electric motor, instead of a belt driven pump. The rest of the system is similar to the previous setup.The motor can be controlled by an ECU. This means we can now vary the flow/pressure to suit all driving conditions.We switch off when not needed to save energy.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
Electro-Hydraulic Power Steering

This precise control reduces power consumption from the engine. It reduces fuel consumption and emissions.More flexible fitment to a wider range of vehicles, no belts and pulleys required. Easier maintenance and reduced costs.Assistance can be momentarily increased to aid parking.

===== SLIDE 11 =====
Electric Power Steering

Reduces power consumption even further. (3%+ fuel savings in tests)Its lighter, more compact and increases “feel” for the driver.ECU controlled and can be programmed to provide different levels of assistance. (speed sensitive)Fails safe with slightly increased input level from driver.

===== SLIDE 12 =====
Electric Power Steering

Can integrate with other systems via CAN. -Electronic Stability Programmes and Self Park Systems.Supplies assistance when needed most such as parking, yet gives increased feel on motorways. It can still provide power assistance with the engine turned off. Great for modern hybrid and is perfect for use on full electric vehicles. The only real disadvantage is cost “if” a fault occurs in the later life of a vehicle.

===== SLIDE 13 =====
Electric Power Steering

EPS is fully ECU controlled. Driver effort is sensed using a torque sensor thats mounted on the column. The ECU looks at this data, along with the road/engine speed via CAN data line. It then supplies varying electrical current to a DC motor. The motor provides the level of assistance required to the column or rack via a small gearbox. The electrical polarity is reversed by the ECU to enable the motor to rotate left and right. Some systems may use angle sensors.
