Introduction

A vehicle must not be driven with a flat tyre, otherwise the wheel and tyre assembly may be damaged.

Knowing how to repair a tyre is an important skill.

Almost any sharp object left on the road can cause a puncture.

General guidelines allow the repair of punctures of up to 6.4 mm in diameter, in a tyre's tread area.

Larger tread punctures, and punctures to the shoulder and sidewall areas, should not be repaired. Ensure you follow BS159 for repairs!

This includes punctures caused by small nails and screws.

The manufacturer has no control over the damage caused by a puncture or the quality of the repair.

Speed Rating of Damaged Tyres

The repaired tyre should therefore be treated as a non-speed rated tyre.

If a speed rated tyre is repaired, the manufacturer will no longer confirm that it is capable of high speeds.

When a tyre is punctured, its structure is compromised.

Continuing to drive on a tyre with a ‘slow’ leak may allow moisture to seep around the sharp object and into the tyre.

This will make it less likely that the tyre can be repaired properly.

‘Slow’ Punctures

If moisture reaches the internal steel and fabric cords used to reinforce the tyre, it may cause rust and loss of strength.

The stability of the other tyres should be checked, as they may also have been damaged by road debris. However, the primary concern is to perform a thorough repair on the punctured tyre.

===== SLIDE 10 =====

Before a repair is carried out, the tyre must be removed from its wheel for full inspection.

Some objects that puncture a tyre may be long enough to cut the tyre's sidewall from the inside.

Tyre Repair Guidelines

Unless the inside of a tyre is inspected for hidden damage, there is a serious risk of repairing a weakened tyre.

To re-establish a watertight seal, the damaged area is ‘cleaned’ with a specially designed cutting drill.

The drill removes rust, and sizes the area to accept the rubber stem of the ‘mushroom’ patch and plug.

Tyre Repair Guidelines

Driving on an improperly repaired tyre is dangerous.

If the vehicle is driven at high speeds, the tyre may suddenly fail, causing a loss of control.

Tyre Failure

The use of an inner tube as a substitute for a proper repair generates additional heat and should not be considered.

It can further damage the tyre and allow its strength to deteriorate over time.
