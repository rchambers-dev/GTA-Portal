===== SLIDE 2 =====
Introduction

Next >

There are very strict rules governing the work that can be done on high voltage (HV) electric vehicles.

These are in place to assist the safety and health of the workforce.

In general, work on the live parts of the electrical system is not permitted, and the live parts should be disabled before work is done on any part of the electrical system.

===== SLIDE 3 =====
Five Safety Rules

These are the rules that should always be observed before work is started.

Next >

The first three rules apply to all work on HV systems. Rules four and five may apply, and have to be judged, on an individual case basis.

1. Isolate

3. Verify that it is not live

2. Safeguard against reconnection

4. Earth and short circuit

5. Shroud and safeguard adjacent live parts

===== SLIDE 4 =====
Isolate

Next >

The actual instructions will be vehicle-specific but are likely to include the following:

Switch off the ignition

Withdraw the service plug

Switch off the main battery switch

Remove fuses

Disconnect any charging plug

===== SLIDE 5 =====
Safeguard Against Reconnection

Next >

Safeguards will include:

Remove the ignition key and prevent unauthorized access to it

Store the service plug away from unauthorized access

Lock or otherwise secure the battery switch to ensure it cannot be reconnected

Follow any other manufacturer-specific recommendations

===== SLIDE 6 =====
Verify that it is not Live

Next >

Even with the HV disconnected, there can still be residual charges in the system, due to devices such as capacitors.

These will discharge over time but, even after waiting fifteen minutes or so, the vehicle must be tested to ensure it is not still live.

Multimeters, even marked to CAT III, should not be used for this task because they have had an unacceptably high rate    of accidents.

===== SLIDE 7 =====
Responsibility

Next >

However, the employer has the highest level of responsibility and is ultimately responsible for safety policy, organization, and facilities.

Employers may appoint people to take responsibility.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
Employers Responsibilities

Employers must produce risk assessments and define protective measures.

Next >

They must provide a suitable area in which to work on HV vehicles.

They will also generate the work instructions for working on HV vehicles.

Most importantly, they are responsible for ensuring the qualifications of the staff working on HV vehicles.

Staff Qualifications

Next >

Certain duties of the employer can be delegated to others within the company.

The employer must ensure that their staff possess the necessary specialist and technical skills.

They must also be physically and mentally suitable for any given task.

===== SLIDE 10 =====
Staff Qualifications

Next >

The rules identify three levels of worker for HV systems.

The highest level is the responsible, skilled, electrical person.

The next level is the skilled electrical person.

The final level is the person who has received electrical instruction.

===== SLIDE 11 =====
Staff Qualifications

Next >

A business does not have to employ people at all these levels.

However, an unskilled and untrained person cannot be used to work on HV systems.

In service workshops, it is likely that there will be several skilled electrical persons.

They will have suitable qualifications, experience of working on HV systems, and they will be familiar with the rules and regulations.

===== SLIDE 12 =====
Staff Qualifications

Next >

There may also be several electrically trained people gaining experience under supervision.

They will only be performing set tasks and having their work monitored.

There may be no responsible skilled person in a workshop environment.
